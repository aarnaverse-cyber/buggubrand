Meet the Jodi

A toddler and his bear

Buggu does what every two-year-old does. Papa Babloo never shouts, never lectures, and always gets down to Buggu's height first.

Buggu

The little one with big feelings

Mint onesie, turquoise mask, orange cap with a monster on it. Buggu wants the toy truck, the swing and the tablet, usually all at once. He's learning to say what he feels before he acts on it.

Papa Babloo

Big bear, soft voice

Red B cap, pastel checked shirt, floral trousers and endless patience. Papa Babloo turns a meltdown into a question: what happened, how do you feel, and what can we do now?

For parents

How every short is built

The same six beats each time, so little ones learn the pattern and start using it themselves.

0:00 to 0:04

The moment

Something small goes wrong. A friend takes a toy, a seed won't sprout, the room is dark.

0:04 to 0:10

Papa reframes

Papa Babloo kneels down and asks instead of telling.

0:10 to 0:15

Buggu names it

Sad, scared, cross, shy. Buggu says the feeling out loud.

0:15 to 0:21

The payoff

He tries the better choice and sees what it does for someone else.

0:21 to 0:26

Happy montage

A few seconds of the good feeling, so it sticks.

0:26 to 0:30

The closing line

Papa Babloo says one sentence, also shown on screen, that you can repeat at home.

Pause at the feeling

Stop when Buggu names his feeling and ask Have you ever felt like that?

Borrow the closing line

The last line is written for real life. Use it the next time the same thing happens at the park.

One a day is plenty

Each short is thirty seconds by design. Watch one together, then go and play.
